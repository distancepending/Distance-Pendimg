**The Pivot**

I’ve been dealing with this pain for the last couple months.

Nothing dramatic. Nothing that completely stopped me from running. Just something that kept hanging around.

And because I *could* keep running, I did.

That works until it doesn’t.

I’m training for a race that’s still more than a year away. There is absolutely nothing I can accomplish by forcing another couple weeks of running right now that’s worth turning a manageable injury into something that takes me out for months.

So I’m finally doing the thing I’m probably worst at:

**Backing off before my body forces me to.**

For the next two weeks, I’m not running.

But I’m also not doing nothing.

I’m pivoting.

The running volume is being replaced with Zone 2 rowing as tolerated. I’m keeping my normal lifting schedule with some modifications, and I’m adding dedicated tendon rehab with the goal of actually fixing the problem instead of continuing to negotiate with it every time I put my shoes on.

The goal isn’t to be really fit in November 2026.

The goal is to stand on a starting line in December 2027 healthy enough to run 100 miles for Mama Joanie.

That changes the math.

Two weeks without running is nothing.

Turning something manageable into a six-month injury because I was too stubborn to stop would be something.

So here’s the new plan.

**35 planned running miles → rowing equivalent + tendon rehab**

**Planned rowing: 350 minutes / 5 hr 50 min**

But even that number comes with an asterisk.

The first rowing session is only a **10–15 minute tolerance test**. If the foot stays calm during the row, afterward, and the following morning, then I’ll start progressing toward the scheduled duration.

I’m not replacing “stubbornly run through it” with “stubbornly row through it.”

**Rehab A — Strength**

- Banded ankle inversion — 3 × 15
- Seated calf raise — 3 × 15, slow
- Short-foot / arch doming — 3 × 10, 5-second hold
- Toe yoga / controlled big-toe movement — 2 × 10
- Straight-knee calf stretch — 2 × 30 sec
- Bent-knee soleus stretch — 2 × 30 sec

**Rehab B — Light Load / Mobility**

- Gentle banded inversion — 2 × 15
- Short-foot / arch doming — 3 × 10, 5-second hold
- Toe yoga — 2 × 10
- Straight-knee calf stretch — 2 × 30 sec
- Bent-knee soleus stretch — 2 × 30 sec

Week 4 is really about one thing:

**Calm it down without shutting everything else down.**

Then, assuming the foot responds well, I start building it back.

**Planned rowing: 430 minutes / 7 hr 10 min**

**Rehab C — Progressive Strength**

This only happens if Week 4 actually settles things down.

- Banded inversion — 3 × 15–20
- Double-leg calf raise — 3 × 12–15 with a slow 3-second lowering
- Progress toward affected-leg-biased calf raise — 3 × 8–12
- Short-foot / arch doming — 3 × 10, 5-second hold
- Single-leg balance — 3 × 30 sec
- Straight-knee calf stretch — 2 × 30 sec
- Bent-knee soleus stretch — 2 × 30 sec

I’m modifying leg days too. No standing calf raises, plyometrics, loaded carries, or lunges/Bulgarians if they reproduce the pain. Pain-free supported leg work stays.

And there’s a rule for all of this:

**Pain stays at 2/10 or less.**

No increasing pain as I go. No noticeable worsening later that day. No waking up the next morning with the foot more pissed off because I decided rehab was another workout I needed to win.

If symptoms increase, I back off.

Same thing with the long row. Saturday calls for 140 minutes, but if sitting there continuously starts irritating the foot, it becomes **2 × 70 minutes**.

Because this isn’t about proving I can suffer.

I already know I can suffer.

This is about learning when suffering actually moves me toward the goal and when it’s just me being stubborn.

There’s a part of me that wants this whole build to look perfect.

Mileage goes up. Fitness goes up. Long runs get longer. Every box gets checked. Eventually I run 100 miles.

But that’s obviously not how the next 14 months are going to work.

There will be injuries.

There will be bad weeks.

There will be missed runs, changed workouts, work, life, weather, exhaustion, and probably a bunch of things I haven’t even thought about yet.

The spreadsheet is going to change.

**The reason doesn’t.**

If getting there means spending two weeks sitting on a rower and doing toe yoga instead of running, then apparently that’s the next step.

**No running. Row. Lift. Rehab. Heal. Come back.**

Still moving forward.

Just not on foot for a couple weeks.
